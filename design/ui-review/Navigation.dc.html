WHOLE-INSTRUMENT · NAVIGATION
Every way in is a chord
Six controls, no labels, no menu key. Everything global is a held-button combination — and when this artboard was first drawn, none of them was discoverable from any screen. That is the part that has since been fixed. Hold A on its own, or Z on its own, and the running app dims behind a card listing what that button modifies.

OPEN
A is overloaded five ways
A alone toggles invert-display in Setup. A+encR opens the switcher. A+encL opens IO settings. A+B does a third thing that differs per app. And A held through the splash, with either B or encR, erases every setting in the module. The same finger position means five things depending on what else is down, where you are, and how long ago the module powered on. On a NORTHERNLIGHT build the splash erase is A+encR — the same two controls, in the same order, as the switcher chord. That is the one destructive gesture reachable by doing the most practised thing in the instrument slightly too early.
SHIPPED
Entry gestures leaked into the screen they opened
Proven, not theoretical: the both-encoder chord left encR held, and the overlay's 250 ms recall timer fired on it — a bus-wide recall nobody asked for. When this artboard was first drawn the fix existed only there, and every other chord-opened screen had the same shape and no guard. It is now one rule at the point screens are entered — see below for exactly what shipped, and for the two places it does not reach yet.
OPEN
A+B means something different in every app
Clock router, screen flip, clock setup. Two of those are clock-related and one is not — so the gesture is almost consistent, which is worse than either extreme. A player who learns it in Captain MIDI gets a flipped screen in Setup.
Shipped since this review
The release-first rule is global. Ui::IgnoreUntilRelease(mask) is armed before each chord-opened screen exists, and every button named in the chord is dead — DOWN, PRESS, LONG_PRESS and LONG_RELEASE alike — until it has been physically released. Armed at all four global entries: the preset overlay, the app switcher, IO settings and the screensaver. The old SetButtonIgnoreMask() is now one way to arm that rule rather than a second mechanism, which retires two of its own failures: it dropped exactly one event per button (so a held button spent the drop on its LONG_PRESS and let the LONG_RELEASE through), and it snapshotted the raw pin (so a chord button released a few milliseconds early was never covered at all). Encoder turns are deliberately untouched, so a screen opened with an encoder held is still navigable by turning it.
Two gaps, stated rather than glossed. (1) The rule filters the event stream. A screen that times a hold by sampling the pin itself never passes through it — and the preset overlay's STORE/RECALL bars still do exactly that, via read_immediate() and their own *_needs_release booleans. read_deliberate() exists for precisely this and currently has no callers, so the per-screen guard is still what stops the phantom recall. (2) The per-app A+B screens arm nothing global. Hemisphere and Quadrants call the old SetButtonIgnoreMask() (which now routes into the rule); Captain MIDI, Calibr8or and Setup call nothing at all.
Also shipped — the chord card
Hold a modifier on its own and it lists what it modifies. 700 ms of A alone — nothing else down — tints the running app to 50% and draws an opaque card titled HOLD A: A+encR: Switch App · A+encL: I/O Cfg · A+B: the gloss for this app (Clock Setup, Clock Router or Flip Screen — no generic label, because there is no true generic answer) · encL+encR: Presets · Z+A: Screensaver. Holding Z gives the Z-side equivalent, including what a bare Z does here. Press-and-hold-the-first-button is the universal discovery move on unlabelled hardware, and it was the one gesture with no feedback at all — which is what left A, X and Y reading as dead buttons.

700 ms is load-bearing. The floor is the 500-tick long-press boundary: below it, letting go after reading the card emits an ordinary A press, and a plain A is a live control in most apps — it changes setup in Captain MIDI, moves the applet selection in Hemisphere, inverts the whole display in Setup. Past 500 the release is a LONG_RELEASE, which nothing in the tree binds. That is what makes "reveal, then let go, and nothing happened" true rather than hoped for. The card's visibility is tested against the live pin, not a latch, because the chords deliberately swallow their own releases — a latch would strand it on screen after every gesture it exists to teach.
What it still does not reach. The card is drawn by the running app, so it is absent from the two screens that are not one: the app switcher and the preset-bus overlay. And it lists no way to erase every setting in the module — the splash gesture is the one chord still written nowhere, which is arguably correct and worth deciding on purpose rather than by omission.
Still proposed — not built
Give A+B one meaning. Two of its three uses are clock setup; the odd one out (screen flip in Setup) is a settings toggle that has a natural home in Setup's own list.
Reach the two screens the card cannot. The app switcher and the preset-bus overlay are drawn by the framework, not by an app, so the chord card never appears over them. Both are places a player arrives at by accident and has to guess their way out of.
Move the factory-erase gesture off the switcher chord on NORTHERNLIGHT builds, or make the two branches agree. Two builds of the same firmware having different destructive gestures is its own hazard — a player who learns one and is handed the other has learned the wrong thing.
Z is on this list because the firmware maps it — but_mid on pin 20, polled as CONTROL_BUTTON_M with the other six. Two attempts to observe the grey clock button firing it on real hardware were inconclusive; the simulator drives its pin as a working control and captions the binding as unconfirmed. If Z is not reachable on this panel, the screensaver chord is unreachable too.
