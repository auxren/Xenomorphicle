Three problems this creates
A is overloaded four ways
A alone toggles invert-display in Setup. A+encR opens the switcher. A+encL opens IO settings. A+B does a third thing that differs per app. The same finger position means four things depending on what else is down and where you are.
Entry gestures leak into the screen they open
Proven, not theoretical: the both-encoder chord left encR held, and the overlay's 250 ms recall timer fired on it — a bus-wide recall nobody asked for. Fixed there by requiring a release first. Every other chord-opened screen has the same shape and none of them guards against it.
A+B means something different in every app
Clock router, screen flip, clock setup. Two of those are clock-related and one is not — so the gesture is almost consistent, which is worse than either extreme. A player who learns it in Captain MIDI gets a flipped screen in Setup.
What I would do about it
Make the release-first rule global, not local. A screen opened by a chord should ignore every button in that chord until it has been released once. One rule, applied at the point screens are entered, retires a whole class of bug rather than the one instance we hit.
Give A+B one meaning. Two of its three uses are clock setup; the odd one out (screen flip in Setup) is a settings toggle that has a natural home in Setup's own list.
Put the chords somewhere. Setup/About is the honest place for a gesture list — it is where a player already goes to find out about the instrument, and it costs no rows anywhere else.
Z is on this list because the firmware maps it, but two attempts to observe the grey clock button firing it on real hardware were inconclusive. If Z is not reachable on this panel, the screensaver chord is unreachable too.
